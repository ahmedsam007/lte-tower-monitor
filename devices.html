Download: how it is measured depends on where the device is connected. The computer running Tower Monitor is measured exactly. For devices on the TP-Link Wi-Fi, the router counts the packets it sends to each one, not the bytes, so the speed is an estimate (marked ≈) that assumes full-size packets and is capped so the total never exceeds the whole-house download. Devices on the Archer Wi-Fi or a cable have no per-device download counter (–); their download is part of the “not split per device” figure on the whole-house tile. Upload comes from the TP-Link's traffic statistics and is exact for every device.

Activity now is packets sent per second. A device that downloads heavily also sends many small reply packets, so this column finds busy devices on the Archer too.

Limit sets a maximum speed for one device on the TP-Link (Bandwidth Control). This router enforces only the upload maximum, so the download is held down indirectly by a low upload cap; the download figure is a target, approximately kept. Block stops one device from reaching the internet (Access Control); it stays connected to the Wi-Fi. Both can be undone here, and nothing on the router changes until you press a button.
